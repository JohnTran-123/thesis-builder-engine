# Honest Scope Disclosure

## The Principle

Every claim in a thesis must be backed by evidence the author has, or labeled as unvalidated. Do not over-claim.

## Three Disclosure Modes

### A. Validated claims — cite the source

For any factual or empirical claim, cite the source. Use the citation style chosen at `/align`. See `rules/no-fabricated-citations.md` for verification requirements.

### B. Author observations — mark explicitly

For claims based on the author's direct experience, mark them so:

- "From the author's field engagement..." (with date / role / context)
- "Based on direct observation during [period at organization]..."
- "The author observed that..."

Do NOT present author observations as universal facts. Frame as situated, contextual evidence.

### C. Unvalidated / aspirational — label honestly

For claims the author cannot verify (e.g., "this approach would scale to other firms"), label them:

- "Aspirational — not validated in this study"
- "Hypothesized — requires empirical testing"
- "Limited scope — extrapolation should be cautious"
- "Author's projection, not measured"

## In Practice

A "lessons" or "recommendations" section should read like this (honest):

> "What worked at [organization] was [specific practice]. **The author cannot claim this generalizes** to other firms without further study. Three conditions appeared necessary for the practice to succeed: [list]. Whether those conditions exist elsewhere is an open question."

NOT like this (dishonest, over-claiming):

> "This practice will work in any firm because [unsupported generalization]."

## Limitations Section Is Mandatory

Every thesis must include an explicit Limitations section that discloses:

- What the methodology cannot show
- Sample size / time / scope constraints
- Assumptions made
- Threats to validity

If a Limitations section is missing or thin (< 1 page for a 50+ page thesis), `/content-review` flags it as HIGH severity.

## When the AI Drafts

When the AI produces drafted content, it must mark its own confidence:

- Validated claims: cite immediately
- Author observations: leave a placeholder for the author to confirm details ("[date / context — author to confirm]")
- Aspirational claims: prefix with the appropriate label

The author then verifies, edits, or removes.
